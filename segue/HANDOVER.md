# Segue – handover (6 November 2026, Segue 0.6)

For the Segue chat. Read this, then the repo's `README.md` and `HANDOVER.md` (how the repo, the build, storage and the other streams work). Segue is internal for now: built to `docs/segue/`, live at https://steviewaring-arch.github.io/ensemble-tools/segue/, and not linked from the index page.

## What it is

Framed loops of images and video, for case studies, social reels and decks – the kind of thing The Brand Identity's motion packs do in After Effects, without After Effects. Media goes into **shapes** (the frames it sits in) on a **canvas**; the shapes move in one of six **kinds** of motion with a **feel**, and everything **loops** exactly. **Slides** – a slide at a time, with a transition and a little movement in each – is the showcase kind, and the GIF writer is built for it (Steve makes showcase GIFs, usually in Photoshop). A background image can sit behind it all and text or an image over it. Captions are optional. Out as MP4, GIF or a PNG of the frame on the stage.

### The kinds and their controls
| Kind | Engine `layout` | Controls (in order) |
|---|---|---|
| **Slides** (was One) | `one` | **Each slide** (Full bleed, As it is, In a shape), **Sizes** (As it is: Fit each, True to scale), **Transition** (Cut, Fade, Dip, Wipe, Push, Cover, Open, Zoom, Through), Direction (only for Wipe, Push, Cover, or a drifting movement), **On screen**, **Transition time** (not for Cut), Feel (only for Push, Cover, Wipe, Open, Zoom – Fade and Dip ease softly, Through has its own curve), **Movement** (None, Zoom in, Zoom out, Drift, Parallax – not for full bleed – Vary), **Amount**, After the last (+ Pull back fold) |
| **Line** | `row` / `column` – Direction picks | Move (Step, Drift, Still), Direction (left/right → row, up/down → column), Rest, Move time / Each shape, Feel, **Lag**, **Keep drifting**, After the last (+ Pull back) |
| **Grid** | `grid` | Columns, Move, Direction (up/down), Rest, Move time / Each shape, Feel, Lag (row by row), Keep drifting |
| **Columns** | `columns` | Columns, Move, Directions (Alternate – down, up, down…, All up, All down), Rest, Move time, Feel, **Out of step**, Keep drifting / Each shape, **Speeds** (Same, Varied), **Offset** |

### Typing values, dragging artwork (0.4)
- **Typing:** `typable()` in `frame.js` turns a slider's value into a button; click, Tab+Enter or Space opens a small field, Enter commits (units stripped, clamped to the slider's min and max, not rounded to its step), Escape cancels, blur commits. `addRange` and the media rows' `itemRange` both use it; a typed value shows as typed.
- **Dragging** (`app.js`, before `setPlaying`): a press on the canvas finds the topmost op under it in the last scene (`cur`, clip-aware), pauses, and drags. Cropped (cover, not As it is): `px`/`py` per item, −100…100% of the picture to spare each way (`drawMedia` places it at `−spare + px/100 × spare`); only an axis with something to spare moves. As it is: `ox`/`oy` in px at a 1080 short side, applied in `oneFinish` with `zm` as the slide's size (those ops are `whole`, so `drawMedia` doesn't crop them). Ctrl or Alt + wheel sets `zm` (cropped 100–400%, as it is 30–200%); double-click resets. On release the item's row opens. Works in every kind where the media fills its shape.

### Layers, the canvas, a clip's In and Out (0.6)
- **Layers** (`slidesOf`, `layerOps` in `engine.js`): in Slides, `plan` and `scene` group the media first – a piece with `join: 'on'` (not the first) joins the last slide before it. The engine works on the bases as before (stops, holds, transitions, movement, captions), then `layerOps` adds each slide's layers beside its op: placed on the slide's **space** (`o.space`, the slide's rect before own-fitting and movement – the canvas for full bleed, the shape, or the whole space for As it is), fitted inside it (`lfit` own) or covering it (`fill`), × `zm`, offset by `ox`/`oy` (px at a 1080 short side, × the slide's scale), then put through the slide's movement (`o.mv`: the scale and shift its movement gave it, from the rect change and `o.inner`; parallax moves the frame only). Behind ones (`place: 'behind'`) go before the base, which then skips its shape colour (`noFill`) so they show through. Keys `s3.f0`, `s3.b0`. In a frame they're clipped to the slide's rect and corners (`lclip`); As it is, not. Layers draw no captions; video in a layer plays from its own Start at as the slide arrives (`since` on each op).
- **Blend and Opacity** (`it.blend`, `it.opa`): in `paint`, a base piece with a blend or under 100% skips its shape's colour and draws its media in `BLEND[...]` at that alpha; layers go through `drawLayer`. `BLEND` gained darken and lighten.
- **In and Out** (`clipTime`): video time is Start at + the time since its shape arrived; with an End at past Start at, it loops between them (`start + mod(d, end − start)`). Columns, Frames and Float use it too. The bar (`trimBlock` in `app.js`): a filmstrip of 8 frames, drawn once from a second video element per item (`probeOf`, `onProbe` queue, `probeAt`), the playing part lit (everything else washed with the card colour), ends and the lit part draggable; In and Out frames redrawn 90 ms after a change; `trims` lets `render` move each bar's tick to the clip's time while it's on the canvas. Changing In or Out brings the piece on screen (`seekTo`) and selects it.
- **The canvas** (`app.js`, before `setPlaying`): `hitAt` (clip-aware, layers included) on hover sets `hoverOp`, outlines it and gives its row `.hover`; a row's mouseenter outlines its piece (`hoverRow`). A press selects (`selId`); without moving it's a click – Media opens (if shut) at the piece's row, scrolled into view (`selectItem`). Dragging: cropped pieces pan `px`/`py` with the whole picture drawn at 32% (`cropBox`); whole pieces (As it is, layers) move `ox`/`oy` by `unitK` – measured by nudging the offset and re-running the scene, so it holds mid-movement – and catch on the guides (`snapMove`: edges and centre within 7 screen px of a margin, column or row edge, the centre or the canvas edge; Alt for free). Corner handles (`handleAt`, 9 screen px) scale `zm` about the centre (`zRange`: layers 5–400, As it is 20–400, crops 100–400). Outlines, handles and guides are drawn by `drawUI` after `paint` in `render` only – never in `frameAt` (exports) – and not in Preview. Esc or a click on nothing lets go. Opening a row takes the playhead to its piece (`momentOf`: in Slides its slide's hold, just after it's arrived; otherwise, of 160 moments, where it's biggest and nearest the middle).
- **Guides** (`guides`, `guM`, `guC`, `guR`, `guG`, `guSnap`): view aids, so in `NOT_LOOK` (not in presets) and never exported; drawn in Difference (white at 60% for lines, 7% for column and row fills) so they show on any work.

### The round 4 kit (0.5 – UI stream)
Segue is where the house UI's next step is tried before Tempo, Rubato and Tutti take it (rules in `shared/UI.md`, last section). `build.py` adds `shared/kit.css` to Segue's styles only. In `frame.js`:
- **Undo** (`UNDO`, `REDO`, `record`, `group`, `pushUndo`, `undo`, `redo`): every `set()` records; the same key within 0.8 s or in one drag (`gesture`) joins up. A preset, a section Reset and removing files push one entry each (`applyPreset`, `resetCard`, `removeItems`, `removePic` in `app.js` – removed files are read back from IndexedDB first so Undo restores them). ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z / Ctrl+Y in `frameStart`, not while typing.
- **What's changed** (`paintChanges`, `addCx`, `resetCtl`, `resetCard`): `baseOf(k)` in `app.js` gives a key's start value – the preset in use (px keys scaled by `unit()`, export keys from its `x`), else `D`. Rows that differ get `.chg` and a `.cx` dot (click to reset); sections and folds get a dot when shut. `edited` is now recomputed from `LOOK` rather than set on any change.
- **Slider** (`upgradeRange`, `syncRange`): relative drag with Shift finer and a catch at the start value (a drag can start on the ?; a click there shows its tip); click jumps; `--z` is the zero point (fills from zero), `--b` the notch; the handle splits only over the label's text and the value. The native range stays for keyboard and tests (pointer events off).
- **Exports** (`eachFrame` in `export.js`): hand the page back every 50 ms rather than every sixth frame, so Stop and the progress keep up with full-size GIFs.
- **Choices** (`addSeg`, `fitSeg`, `placeHL`, `segRO`): a hidden `select.seg-menu` mirrors each seg; `.as-menu` swaps them when the widest label (Medium, 12.5 px) plus 16 px won't fit an equal share. `.hl` is the white: it slides only when the choice changes, and snaps when the row resizes.
- **Steppers** (`addStepper`): `addRange` uses one for whole counts of ten or fewer with no unit.
- **Chips** (`CHIP_ICON`, `chipIcon`): Shuffle, Clear all, Whole clip, Reset, Replace, Stop and "+ …" get an icon; the words stay as the button's text (the tests read it).
- Section titles stay the first span in `.card-toggle` (the tests read them); the number comes after in the markup and is placed first with CSS.

### How the loop works (`segue/engine.js`)
- **One (Slides), Line, Grid** – as 0.1: shapes on a track (or a grid scrolling a row at a time); a camera visits each stop, rest then move with the feel. Loop = shapes × rest + moves (+ pull back), or shapes × Each shape for Drift. In Slides each stop's rest is that slide's own On screen if it has one (`holdOf` in `plan`).
- **Slides – framing** (`oneFinish`, run after every Slides scene): Full bleed makes the shape the whole canvas and switches off corners, border, edge, nudge and caption room (captions under or above become On it); As it is makes the shape the whole space (Size %) and fits each slide's own aspect inside it – True to scale shrinks each by one common scale (`G.rel`), and captions under or above pin the slide to that edge so they line up.
- **Slides – movement** runs from the moment a slide starts arriving (`P.arrive`) to the moment it's gone (`P.gone`), evenly (`uOf`, by track position so a slide arriving and the same slide leaving don't share a value). Zoom in/out scale by 1 → 1 + Amount; Drift moves Amount across (a half each side of centre) the way Direction says; Parallax moves the frame that way and the picture the other, with the picture overscanned by Amount; Vary takes turns by slide (in, drift, out, back). As it is moves the rect; full bleed and In a shape set `o.inner` (scale and offset inside the clip). A single slide becomes a still plan and its movement goes in and back out (cosine) across the loop. In the pull back, slide 0 waits at its start and the last carries on.
- **Slides – transitions** (`oneMove`): Dip fades this one out over the first half and the next up over the second; Wipe gives each a clip either side of an edge crossing the canvas; Open clips the next to a centred window growing from nothing (canvas-shaped) and this one to outside it (`clip.out`, even-odd); Through grows the next from 12% in scale on `THROUGH` while this one grows 18%; wherever the next won't exactly cover this one (As it is, or this one grown), this one fades as it goes.
- **Pull back – Stagger and Float** (`pullScene`): each shape has its own progress through Out and In – the segment's raw progress delayed by `0.6 × Stagger × distance from the focus` (Out; `1 − distance` for In), stretched so all finish together, then eased with the pull back's Feel; neighbours travel into the grid on it, the rest fade on it, and those fading up start up to 35% further out from the focus. Float adds an offset of up to 4.5% of a shape's short side, `sin` of loop time on two incommensurate rates with a phase per shape, scaled by that shape's progress – so both are nothing at the start of Out and the end of In.
- **Lag:** each shape's step is read a beat late – `stepAbs(t − lag × rank)` – rank counted from the leading edge of what's visible, so the one ahead sets off first and the one coming in last. The reference stop for the ranks only changes once every shape has landed, so nothing jumps; the beat is capped (`0.9 × rest ÷ shapes in view`) so the last lands before the next move. Lag is shown in the Lag control but takes from the rest.
- **Keep drifting:** the camera is `(1 − k) × the step path + k × a steady drift`, the drift advancing a stop each rest-and-move, so the two meet at every stop and the loop stays exact. Not in the last rest before a pull back.
- **Columns:** each column runs through all the media from its own starting point (`round(N × c / C)`), so the loop is N steps (Step) or N × Each shape (Drift; Varied doubles it, laps 2, 3, 4). Out of step offsets each column's clock; Offset shifts every other column's start by part of a shape. Heights per shape when the shape is the media's own.
- **Frames:** fixed rectangles (Across × Down, fitted inside Size); frame f shows media f, f + F, f + 2F… (N ÷ gcd(N, F) entries), changing every rest + move, each frame Lag later. Push and cover slide the media inside the frame (`slide` on the op); fade crossfades; captions crossfade at the halfway point. Video in a frame plays from its start point as it arrives.
- **Float:** one, or three, depths (Depth > 0), sized 1 − 0.6 × Depth for the farthest. Each depth is a strip of K places; its length is in proportion to its size, and every strip goes round once a loop – so speed is in proportion to size and everything joins up. K is enough places that the farthest strip covers the canvas with nothing shown twice. Places are scattered across by a seeded hash (Shuffle changes the seed). Step moves the whole field a place at a time.
- **Feels:** Glide (ease in and out), **Soft** (`bezier(.42,0,.58,1)`, the pull back's default from 0.2), **Coast** (`bezier(.18,0,.02,1)`), Inertia, Spring, Linear.
- **Drawing order:** background colour, background image, shapes (each: border, inner clip, behind colour, media – slid within the frame if Frames is changing – edge), captions, fixed caption, overlay. In Float with Sits among the shapes, the overlay is drawn before the nearest depth (`SC.mid`).
- Every op carries a `key` (its place: `t12`, `g3.1`, `c0.4`, `l2.7`), so tests can follow a shape from frame to frame.
- **Exports** draw every frame at full size at its own moment, seeking each video first – as 0.1.

## Crisp media (0.4.3 – `drawCrisp`, `halved`, `sharpenOn` in `app.js`)
- **0.4.1's diagnosis was half right.** Shrinking in one go is soft (fixed then with stepped halving and a sharpen), but the bigger loss came after: the crisp result was drawn at the layout's fractional position (x + 0.6 px), which the browser resamples bilinearly – a second blur – and enlarged media went through a plain bilinear stretch. Measured 5 Nov with fine type (Inter 15/24 px on a 1920 × 1200 screen) against a Lanczos resample, edge detail kept in 4:5: Card stack 55–65%, Phone swipe 63–73%, Push and drift 70–87%, Straight cuts (16:10 enlarged 1.125×) 79%.
- **Now:** when exporting (or paused) and the piece isn't turned or flipped, `drawCrisp` works out where it lands in device pixels. The same size as its own pixels: copied straight onto the grid (snapped under half a pixel – nothing resampled). Otherwise one resample with the fraction of a pixel built in: `halved` steps it down to within 2× (kept for stills, `halfCache`), it's drawn to its exact size and position on a canvas lined up with the grid (`imageSmoothingQuality` high), `sharpenOn` sharpens (1-2-1 blur in two passes; half strength shrunk, a third enlarged, none near 1:1; the outer two pixels and anything see-through left alone; Lift washed-out colours folded in), and it's copied on 1:1 with the transform reset (clip and alpha still apply). A still at rest keeps its result (`onGrid`, per piece, keyed by size and fraction), so a held slide costs nothing after its first frame. Turned media keeps 0.4.1's `crispOf`.
- **After:** Card stack 91–92%, Phone swipe 83–86%, Push and drift 98%, Straight cuts 93%; the small-scale grids (Rows up, Columns, Posters) 117–120% – a touch sharper than Lanczos, no halos by eye. Against Lanczos at the same sub-pixel place, 37.6 levels RMS (plain) → 13.9. Cost: about 90 ms a frame more for full-frame video on the test machine (video seeking is 350+ ms there either way).
- **Bitrate:** `MP4_Q` 16/32/50 Mbps at 1080 × 1350, 30 fps, × area × fps/30; `contentHint: 'detail'`. Encoders spend what the picture needs up to that, so the Export sheet says "up to".
- **Left as it is:** enlarged media is softer than its source however it's drawn – a 16:10 or 16:9 clip filling 4:5 is enlarged 1.125–1.25×. Colour at half size is dealt with below.
- **Not done:** snapping pieces at rest to whole pixels (Lanczos at x + 0.5 is itself softer than at x + 0; it would need each piece's rest known for the frame either side, so the loop point doesn't shimmer) – backlog.

## Colour edges in MP4 (0.4.4 – `sharpChroma`, `spsExport` in `export.js`)
- **The finding:** Steve's 0.4.3 export of Numeral 01 Colour (magenta 22,41 on orange; green on pale grey in another saver) was hazy at the edges of the figures. H.264 keeps colour at half size each way (4:2:0); where two colours differ mostly in hue, not brightness, the edge is carried by colour alone. Segue's `toI420` averaged each 2 × 2 block, which players rebuild softly. Measured with his clip at 18.5 s in a card (0.45×): the drawn frame matches a Lanczos resample of the source (3.5 levels RMS); the decoded MP4 was 8.0 from the drawn frame. Not the encoder – on Steve's iMac the hardware encoder keeps colour at 64 dB on magenta/orange and green/grey type (measured through the Claude app's browser).
- **Tried (modelled in numpy against both kinds of player – those that rebuild colour from the middle of each block, and those that assume H.264's default, the left of each pair):** the MPEG-2 [1,2,1] filter, left-sited (better for left players, worse for the others); refining the colour samples against the rebuild; correcting brightness fully (crisper but a dark outline round the figures); halfway (a faint edge only). Chosen: centre-sited (as before), refined three times, brightness halfway, and the stream labelled centre – 5.2 for centre players, 7.5 for left ones (0.4.3: 7.8 and 9.0).
- **How:** after `toI420` (unchanged – flat blocks keep their exact colour), `sharpChroma` finds the colour samples whose 3 × 3 neighbours differ by more than 4 levels; for those only, three Jacobi steps of c += adjoint(target − bilinear rebuild) over each sample's 4 × 4 footprint, rounded and clamped; then each pixel in those footprints gets Y + ½(Y* − Y), where Y* is the brightness that minimises the RGB error given the rebuilt colour. `spsExport` rewrites each SPS (in the avcC and in the stream) bit by bit: video range as before, and `chroma_loc_info_present_flag` = 1 with both types 1 (centre) – adding a VUI if there isn't one. `spsVideoRange` (sources) is unchanged. `S.xChroma = false` turns it off (tests only).
- **Result:** Steve's 22,41 through the whole export (encoded, decoded by ffmpeg): 8.0 → 5.5 levels; visibly clean edges. About 20 ms a frame more on the test machine.
- **2× (Export › Size):** `outSize()`; `frameAt(c,x,W,H,t,k)` lays out at 1× and paints at k (the engine's own `scale`), so nothing in the design moves; `drawCrisp` sees the transform and resamples media at 2×. The 2× colour is as fine as a 1× file's pixels – 2.5 levels from the drawn frame shown at 1080 wide. Encoder: level 5.2 (`avc1.640034`) first when a frame passes level 5.1's limits; `pickEncoder` tests at no more than 1080 × 1350's area at the same bitrate per pixel, then uses the setup at full size. Steve's Mac takes up to 3840 × 2160 and 2160 × 3840 in hardware. MP4 and PNG only (GIF has its own sizes).

## Which encoder (0.4.2, test footage 0.4.3 – `pickEncoder` in `export.js`)
- **Safari:** Steve's renders at 16, 32 and 50 Mbps were all 16.5 MB on his Intel iMac. WebKit's H.264 WebCodecs encoder is libwebrtc's VideoToolbox one – made for video calls – and settles its own quality. Some Safari versions stall for good in `latencyMode: 'quality'` (mediabunny #541). Chrome's hardware encoder ignores the bitrate in variable mode on some Macs (w3c/webcodecs#430).
- **Chrome on Steve's iMac** (Chrome 152, measured 5 Nov through the Claude desktop app's built-in browser, which is the same Chromium on the same Mac): H.264 in hardware and software, with constant, variable and quantizer modes in hardware (VP9 and AV1 software only). Busy test footage at 1080 × 1350: hardware 32 Mbps (variable or constant) → 32.8 Mbps, 52 dB; 8 Mbps → 38 dB; software 32 Mbps → 46 dB. A full Segue export of flat, promo-like clips (Straight cuts): 1.9 Mbps and 56–59 dB from the frames Segue drew – the encoder isn't what softened Steve's export. `pickEncoder` chose hardware/constant there.
- **What Segue does:** `encoderSetups` lists the H.264 setups the browser takes (`ENC_TRY`: hardware/constant, software/variable, software/constant, hardware/variable, no preference, then a real-time variant; VP9 if there's no H.264). `pickEncoder` encodes `sampleFrames` – 24 frames of a fixed, detailed texture zooming 0.4% a frame and drifting 1.37 px, counted from the 8th so rate control has settled – at the bitrate asked for and at a third of it, and keeps the first setup whose bytes grow by more than 15%. (0.4.2 panned it by whole pixels – predicted almost for nothing, so even a following encoder grew only 1.3×; fresh noise every frame was no use either.) Here: 3.1× for 3×, landing on the target. Each try has 4 s of queue stall or 8 s in all; the real export stops after 15 s of stall. `encUsed` keeps the result; the console logs it after each MP4.
- **In Safari:** nothing follows, so the toast and the Export sheet say Quality makes no difference there and Chrome makes the crisper MP4. The way to parity is an encoder of our own (OpenH264 in WebAssembly, BSD; Cisco's patent cover is for its own binaries only) – Steve's decision, in the backlog.
- **Quantizer mode** (a fixed quality per frame, `avc: {quantizer}`) works in hardware on Steve's Mac – a fallback if a browser's rate control ever misbehaves; not used.

## GIFs (`makeGIF` in `export.js`)
Built for showcase GIFs. Each frame is drawn at full size, scaled to the GIF size, and compared with what the GIF is showing (per pixel, in source colours; within 2 levels counts as unchanged, which soaks up video noise without drifting, because the comparison is with what's showing, not the last frame):
- **Nothing changed:** nothing is written; the frame before is held longer. Delays are worked out from each frame's moment, so the GIF's length is the loop's length exactly.
- **Something changed:** only the rectangle round the change is written (disposal "leave in place"), with the unchanged pixels inside it transparent (index 255), so the frame before shows through exactly.
- **Colours** per frame or part: median cut over 15-bit bins, but each palette colour is the **true average** of its pixels, so a flat colour is exact (0.2 rounded #F5F5F3 to 247, 247, 247). **Dither** (Floyd–Steinberg, changed pixels only) is optional.
- Sizes measured 5 Nov at 4:5, half size, with eight photographs (stills from the zoom-through recording): Straight cuts 0.85 MB (9.6 s loop); with movement every pixel changes every frame, so Zoom through is 12.1 MB at 20 fps and 9.0 MB at 15 (5.2 s), Open and zoom 20.6/16.7 MB (11.6 s), Push and drift 16.6/12.9 MB (19.2 s), Slow fades 45.7/36.3 MB (24.8 s). The Export sheet says so when anything moves. Lossy LZW (as gifsicle's `--lossy`) is the next lever – see the backlog.
