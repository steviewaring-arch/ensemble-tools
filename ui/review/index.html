One interface for the tools

How Tutti, Rubato and Tempo look and work today, and a few ways to refine them into one system. Same typeface, same monochrome, same pills. Tidier underneath, shorter on the surface.

8 October 2026Tutti 6.0 · Rubato 1.0 · Tempo 0.7Measured in Chromium at 1440 × 900

The short version

The language is right. The system isn’t, yet.

Tutti set a good house style: one weight of Aktiv Grotesk, tight tracking, monochrome, pills and round ticks. As the tools grew, each new need got its own size, height or fold, so most ideas now exist in three or four versions. The panels show the cost. Tempo’s is nearly six screens long and opens with every card open.

The fix is mostly subtraction: four type sizes, one control height, one way to fold, and closed sections that still tell you what’s set. On top of that sits a choice about layout. I’ve drafted three below and recommend the two-panel split you suggested, with the panels ruled rather than boxed.

Keep

  • One weight of Aktiv Grotesk – hierarchy from size and grey alone is very Swiss
  • Monochrome – the only colour on screen is the work
  • Pills, round ticks, hairlines – a recognisable kit
  • Double-click to reset – quiet and useful
  • The stage capsule – the right home for play, scrub and export
  • Remembered folds, widths and theme

Fix

  • Closed cards say nothing – a title and a “+”
  • Sliders take two rows – about 70 px each
  • Choices wrap raggedly – 2 + 2, 6 + 1, 4 + 1
  • Two segmented styles – filled pills and raised tabs
  • Export lives in three places – a card, a card and a capsule, a capsule
  • Stage chips are sentences – “Preview at 1440 × 900, the shape of…”

How depth worksPrimary, secondary and tertiary – one rule for each

1

Section

The panel’s chapters: Fonts, Clock, Colour. A title, and when shut, one line saying what’s set – “24-hour · Comma · No seconds”. Open as many as you like; each remembers.

Replaces the card. Shut sections read as a list, so a long panel becomes a short one without hiding anything.

2

Group

The part of a section you don’t always need: Mixing, Your colours, More. A small grey label over a hairline, with its own summary. Shut by default once a section runs past a screen.

Replaces the fold, Rubato’s More and the sub-heads. Same chevron as a section, smaller voice.

3

Detail

Belongs to one row or one control: a font’s role and tracking, how to install a screen saver. Opens in place, in a sunken well under the thing it belongs to.

Things that change nothing – a tooltip, Randomise’s options, a colour picker – float instead, and close when you click away.

–

Three levels, never four. Only the panel scrolls. Anything shut tells you what’s inside it. Controls that depend on something switched off disappear, and the space closes up.

Decisions for youMy recommendation first. Change any of them.

  1. Layout – B, two panels

    Right panel folds into a stage capsule under 1,180 px wide. A is the safe fallback. C is the most beautiful for Tempo alone, and the worst for Tutti’s embed.

  2. Panels – ruled, not boxed

    Sections separated by hairlines across the panel; fills kept for controls and anything that floats. This is the one real change in look, and the most Swiss. Cards is the safe refinement of today.

  3. Size – 15 px base

    Down from 16.75, with 32 px controls. Check it on your 13-inch before we commit – flip Size in the mock-up to compare.

  4. Corners – 20, 12, 8 and round

    Frame, cards, inner things, controls. Close to today’s 22 and 12, but nested so inner corners sit inside outer ones.

  5. Help – a tooltip on every control, the “?” goes

    Hover for a beat or tab to a control and its tip appears; Tempo loses 22 small circles. The “?” stays on touch screens. Your “tooltips by default”, without the clutter. Paragraph hints become one line, with longer instructions in a Detail.

  6. Theme – follow the computer, then remember

    First visit opens in the system’s light or dark; after that each tool remembers. Today all three force dark.

  7. Header – one line

    Name, version, By ENSEMBLE, at 22 px. Saves about 50 px at the top of every panel.

Once these are settled I’ll build tokens v2, the component page at /ui/ and UI.md for your sign-off, then move Tempo, Rubato and Tutti across one at a time, with the parity and app tests run and before-and-after screenshots for each. Labels stay word for word, so the tests still find every control.

Shown here in Helvetica Neue or Arial – this page can’t load the Adobe Fonts kit, so judge size, spacing and rhythm rather than letterforms. The tools themselves use Aktiv Grotesk, which sets a touch wider. Panel content is Tempo 0.7’s real labels; the fonts and presets in it are examples.
